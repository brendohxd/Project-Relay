Protocol in motion

From a question to a defensible decision.

Relay does not ask every participant to agree. It asks them to leave enough evidence for the next participant—and the final authority—to understand exactly what happened.

  1. 01Bound the task

    Declare one question, inputs, constraints, risks, and acceptance criteria.

  2. 02Submit evidence

    Attach methods, commands, artifacts, failures, hashes, and context.

  3. 03Review independently

    Reproduce where policy requires it and preserve disagreement.

  4. 04Record authority

    A named person accepts, remediates, rejects, or defers.

One protocol, many profiles

Where accountable reasoning matters.

Profiles can change evidence requirements, review independence, risk thresholds, and authority without changing Relay’s underlying record.

01Scientific research+

Claims, methods, independent reproduction, adversarial review, remediation, and named scientific authority.

Scientific acceptance remains with the relevant community and canon.
02ITSM validation+

Bound operational questions, reproducible checks, explicit exceptions, and accountable service decisions.

Workflow readiness only; ITSM scientific claims remain separate.
03Education and assessment+

Rubric-based marking, moderation, appeals, and consistency checks with teacher authority.

Assessment assistance, never autonomous academic judgement.
04Hiring assistance+

Evidence against declared criteria, consistency checks, review trails, and accountable decisions.

No autonomous hiring decisions or opaque ranking.
05Incident investigation+

Timelines, artifacts, competing explanations, remediation evidence, and post-incident decisions.

Useful across engineering, safety, security, and operations.
06Audit and due diligence+

Declared evidence, artifact verification, visible gaps, exceptions, and accepted residual risk.

Applicable to compliance, grants, procurement, and policy review.

Trust boundary

Integrity is not truth.

Relay can establish
  • A record follows a declared structure
  • An artifact matches its hash
  • Required reviews and reproductions exist
  • Disagreement and remediation were preserved
  • Who exercised decision authority
Relay cannot establish by itself
  • A claim is scientifically true
  • A method is valid merely because it ran
  • Consensus is correct
  • An AI output deserves authority
  • Governance can be replaced by software
ITSM proving-ground boundary

Relay may publish workflow readiness and gate progress. ITSM equations, datasets, results, manuscripts, and scientific acceptance remain in the separate ITSM repository and knowledge base.
